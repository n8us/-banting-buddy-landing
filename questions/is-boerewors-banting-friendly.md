**Short answer:** It depends on the filler. Traditional, pure boerewors made with meat, fat, vinegar, and spices is on the **Green list** (~1–3 g net carbs per 100 g). But commercial wors bulked out with rusk, wheat cereal, or sugar is on the **Red list** and can easily pack 8–12 g net carbs per serving.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Clean boerewors (meat + spices only) | 1.5–3 g | 0 g | 1.5–3 g | 20–25 g | 15 g | Green (Freely) |
| Commercial braaiwors (rusk/cereal/sugar) | 8–12 g | 0.5 g | 7.5–11.5 g | 16 g | 11 g | Red (Avoid) |

A 100 g braai piece of clean boerewors has about 2–3 g total carbs, 0 g fibre and **2–3 g net carbs**.

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026. Filler figures from SA label checks, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

By law in South Africa, authentic boerewors must contain at least 90% meat, but that remaining 10% is where carbs hide. Rusk (essentially dried, toasted wheat bread), cereal meal, dextrose, and sweet spice glazes are the usual culprits. Supermarket "braaiwors" or "economy wors" doesn't have to meet the 90% meat minimum and is often packed with cereal filler and vegetable starch. I scanned a budget braaiwors at Pick n Pay with Banting Buddy and it flagged cereal filler plus dextrose — 9 g glycaemic carbs per 100 g compared to 2 g for the clean butcher's boerewors right next to it. Same fridge, very different food.

Flavours matter too. Plain beef, pork-and-beef, and game wors are usually safest. Anything labelled honey, sweet chilli, chutney, or "sticky BBQ" almost always carries added sugar. Cheese wors can be fine carb-wise, but check for cereal rusk all the same — cheese doesn't replace the grain binder.

Your best move is buying from a quality butcher who makes "gluten-free / zero-rusk" boerewors, or checking pre-packs at Checkers and Pick n Pay (re-checked labels Sep 2026). Look for: beef/pork/lamb, animal fat, salt, roasted coriander, black pepper, cloves, nutmeg, and vinegar. If you see rusk, wheat flour, cereal, modified maize starch, or sugar high up the list, skip it.

Portions are generous at a braai. Two decent pieces of clean wors (about 200 g) cost only ~4–5 g net carbs — it's the braaibroodjies, pap, sweet marinades, and beer that wreck the day, not the meat.

## What to eat instead

If you can't find clean boerewors without rusk, lamb chops, chicken thighs with skin, pork rashers, or ribeye steak are all 100% Green list braai staples with 0g carbs. Keep it simple: meat, coarse salt, braai fire.
