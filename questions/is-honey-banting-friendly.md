**Short answer:** No. In Banting (*The Real Meal Revolution*), honey sits strictly on the **Red list** (foods to avoid). Honey is roughly 82 g net carbs per 100 g, so even a single teaspoon costs you 5.7 g net carbs — being raw, local, or organic doesn't change how your body processes the sugar.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Honey | 82 g | 0 g | 82 g | 0 g | 0.3 g | Red (Strictly Avoid) |

One teaspoon (7 g) has about 5.7 g total carbs, 0 g fibre and **5.7 g net carbs**. A tablespoon (21 g) costs about **17 g net carbs**.

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

The "natural" halo is what catches people. Fynbos, raw, cold-extracted, organic — lovely words, same sugar. Honey is roughly 80% fructose and glucose with almost no fibre, so it hits your blood sugar like table sugar. A drizzle over yoghurt, two spoons in rooibos, honey-glazed nuts: each "little bit" is 6–12 g carbs, and most of us don't stop at one.

SA shelves make it tempting. A 500 g bottle of local honey goes for R100–R180 at Woolies, Checkers or the weekend market (prices seen Sep 2026), and the label talks about bees and fynbos, not carbs. Some "honey-style" syrups are worse — cut with glucose syrup and flavouring. I pointed Banting Buddy at a squeezy "honey blend" at Spar and it flagged added glucose syrup on top of the honey itself. If the label says blend, put it back.

You'll also find honey hiding in banting-unfriendly places: health bars, "sugar-free" rusks sweetened with honey, salad dressings, marinades and biltong glaze. Honey is still sugar on an ingredients list, wherever it sits. For strict banting days I'd skip it completely rather than trying to micro-dose it — a teaspoon here and there keeps the sweet craving alive without ever satisfying it.

## What to eat instead

For sweetness, xylitol, erythritol, and pure stevia are the standard Banting swaps (categorised on the Light Red list for occasional use) at near-zero net carbs — xylitol measures like sugar for baking, erythritol stays grainy in cold drinks. A teaspoon of either in tea costs you nothing worth counting. If you want honey flavour in a dressing, a drop of vanilla plus your usual sweetener gets you close enough. And if the craving is really about comfort, a block of 85% dark chocolate (about R40) scratches it for ~3 g carbs.
