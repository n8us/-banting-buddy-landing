**Short answer:** No. In Banting (*The Real Meal Revolution*), oats sit squarely on the **Red list** (all grains and cereals are strictly avoided). Raw oats pack about 56 g net carbs per 100 g, and even a small bowl of cooked oats costs roughly 25 g net carbs — far too dense in starch for Banting.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Oats, raw dry | 66 g | 10 g | 56 g | 7 g | 13 g | Red (Strictly Avoid) |
| Cooked oats (water) | 12 g | 1.7 g | 10.3 g | 1.4 g | 2.5 g | Red (Strictly Avoid) |

Cooked oats (100 g, plain, made with water) have about 12 g total carbs, 1.7 g fibre and **10.3 g net carbs**. A typical 250 g bowl lands around **25 g net carbs**, before you add milk, honey, or fruit.

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

Oats carry an enormous health halo in South Africa — Jungle Oats has been a pantry staple for generations, about R60–R80 for 1 kg at Checkers and Pick n Pay (Sep 2026). The "heart-healthy" fibre arguments don't change human biochemistry: ten grams of fibre cannot cancel out fifty-six grams of digestible starch when your daily Banting target is 25–50 g net. Instant sachets are even worse, with added sugars pushing carb counts past 70 g per 100 g dry weight.

Toppings compound the problem. The classic South African breakfast bowl — oats simmered in milk, drizzled with honey, and topped with sliced banana — easily exceeds 50–60 g net carbs in a single meal. That exhausts an entire day's Banting carb budget before 8 am, leading to a blood sugar spike followed by intense mid-morning hunger.

Overnight oats, steel-cut oats, oat bran, and oat milk all stay firmly on the Red list. Oat milk in particular deserves caution: at 7–10 g carbs per 100 ml, it is more carb-dense than cow's milk and spikes insulin quickly.

## What to eat instead

For a warm bowl breakfast, chia-and-flax porridge or coconut porridge is the direct Banting swap — around 2–3 g net per bowl when made with coconut milk and topped with cinnamon and cream. Eggs are the most nutritious and economical staple: two eggs fried in real butter cost under 1 g net carb and provide satiety for hours. Plain Greek yoghurt with a spoonful of almond butter and fresh strawberries lands around 6–8 g net carbs.
