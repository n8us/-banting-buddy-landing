**Short answer:** Yes. Plain beef or game biltong sits proudly on the **Green list** in Banting (*The Real Meal Revolution*). It has around 2–4 g net carbs per 100 g and roughly 50 g protein, making it South Africa's premier low-carb whole-food snack.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Biltong, beef, plain (with fat) | 2–4 g | 0 g | 2–4 g | 12–18 g | 45–50 g | Green (Freely) |
| Sweet chilli / BBQ biltong | 12–18 g | 0 g | 12–18 g | 6 g | 40 g | Red (Avoid) |

A 30 g snack pack of plain biltong has about 0.6–1.2 g total carbs, 0 g fibre and **~1 g net carbs**.

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026. Carb range from SA label checks, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

Biltong in its authentic form is just beef, coarse salt, roasted coriander, black pepper, and vinegar. In Banting, **fatty biltong ("geelvet") is actively preferred** over ultra-lean biltong because healthy animal fat provides sustained energy and prevents over-consuming lean protein.

The danger lies entirely in sweet marinades and bastings. Honey-glazed, BBQ, sweet chilli, chutney, and "sticky" biltong can hit 10–18 g carbs per 100 g — four times the carb count of traditional biltong. I ran a pre-packed sweet-chilli stick through Banting Buddy at Checkers and it came out at 12 g glycaemic carbs per 100 g, with brown sugar and dextrose listed high in the ingredients.

Game (kudu, springbok, gemsbok) and ostrich biltong follow the same rules: plain is Green list, sweet coatings are Red list. Game meat is naturally leaner, so pair it with some avocado or a slice of cheddar cheese to keep your healthy fat intake up.

Check the ingredients when buying pre-packed bags at Woolworths, Checkers, or Pick n Pay. If brown sugar, glucose, maltodextrin, or chutney seasonings appear near the top, choose the plain, traditional fatty cuts instead.

## What to eat instead

Droëwors made without cereal or rusk filler is the closest swap — similar carbs, rich in beef fat, and easy to keep in the car. Raw or dry-roasted nuts (macadamias, walnuts) work for variety, though they sit on the Orange list and should be portioned to ~30g. Plain salted pork crackling is another zero-carb Green list staple.
