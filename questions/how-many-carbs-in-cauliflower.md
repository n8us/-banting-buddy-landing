**Short answer:** Very few — about 3 g net carbs per 100 g. In Banting (*The Real Meal Revolution*), cauliflower is a premier **Green list** vegetable (<5 g carbs per 100 g: eat freely to satiety). One cup (107 g) costs roughly 3.2 g net carbs, making it the supreme low-carb substitute for mash, rice, and potato bakes.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Cauliflower, raw | 5 g | 2 g | 3 g | 0.3 g | 1.9 g | Green (Freely) |

One cup chopped (107 g) has about 5.4 g total carbs, 2.1 g fibre and **3.2 g net carbs**.

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

Fresh heads are the safest and most economical buy — around R30–R45 a head at Pick n Pay and Checkers, and often less at local green-grocers (Sep 2026). Frozen florets are equally good carb-wise and convenient with zero trimming waste. 

Where it gets tricky is commercial pre-made "cauli" convenience products. I scanned a frozen cauliflower cheese bake in Banting Buddy that looked compliant on the front, but the cheese sauce was thickened with wheat flour, jumping to 9 g carbs per 100 g. Supermarket cauli pizza bases and pre-seasoned cauli rice packets also frequently sneak in rice flour, potato starch, or dextrose. Always scan the barcode or check the ingredient list.

Cooking method shifts texture more than macros:
- **For cauli mash:** The classic beginner error is under-draining. Steam or boil florets until tender, drain thoroughly, let the hot pot steam-dry the florets for two minutes, then blend with real butter, cream cheese, salt, and black pepper.
- **For cauli rice:** Blitz raw florets coarsely in a food processor, then sauté quickly in butter or lard over high heat so it crisps rather than steams soggy.

## What to eat instead

Broccoli is the closest Green list cousin at ~4 g net per 100 g. Baby marrows (courgettes/zucchini) run ~2 g net carbs and make wonderful noodles or pan-fries. Cabbage (about 3.5 g net) is the ultimate budget Green list hero: roast cabbage wedges with butter and coarse salt for an easy weeknight braai side.
