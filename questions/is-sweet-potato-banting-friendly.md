**Short answer:** Not on strict weight-loss days. In Banting (*The Real Meal Revolution*), sweet potato sits on the **Orange list** (6–25g carbs per 100g: consume in small, measured portions, mainly for weight maintenance or active individuals). Boiled sweet potato has about 17 g net carbs per 100 g, so a normal 150 g serve costs 25.5 g — your entire carb allowance for the day in one side dish.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Sweet potato, boiled | 20 g | 3 g | 17 g | 0.1 g | 1.6 g | Orange (Caution) |
| White potato, boiled | 17 g | 1.8 g | 15.2 g | 0.1 g | 2.0 g | Red (Avoid) |

A 150 g serve (about one small sweet potato) has about 30 g total carbs, 4.5 g fibre and **25.5 g net carbs**.

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

Sweet potatoes sit on the Orange list, whereas regular white potatoes are strictly Red list. While sweet potato is a whole food rich in micronutrients with a lower glycaemic impact than white potato or refined starch, your body still metabolises the ~17 g of starch per 100 g into glucose. Roasting concentrates the sugars further as water evaporates, pushing roasted sweet potato cubes toward 20 g net per 100 g.

Portion size decides everything here. Fifty grams cubed into a stew (about 8.5 g net) can squeak into a lenient day if everything else is clean. A whole baked sweet potato the size of your fist with butter and cinnamon — easily 250 g, 42 g net — blows the day apart on its own. Restaurant portions are the worst: that honey-glazed wedge pile next to your steak can top 50 g carbs before you've touched the starter. I logged a Spur-style veg side in Banting Buddy after one family dinner and the sweet potato alone beat my whole day's target. Lesson learned.

At Pick n Pay and Woolies, pre-cut trays and frozen mash (around R35–R55, seen Sep 2026) sometimes carry added sauces or brown-sugar seasoning, so read the pack. Plain whole sweet potatoes are cheap — roughly R25–R40 per kg — which makes them tempting budget filler. They fill the plate, just not your macros.

If you're maintaining weight or training intensely, a small portion once or twice a week is fine. For strict weight-loss weeks (aiming under 25–50 g net carbs daily), skip it and spend those carbs on Green list vegetables you can eat a full plate of.

## What to eat instead

Cauliflower mash is the classic stand-in at 3 g net per 100 g — boil it soft, drain it properly, then mash with butter and salt. Roast pumpkin (butternut runs ~10 g net per 100 g, so keep it to 100 g) scratches the sweet-orange itch for fewer carbs. For stews, baby marrows and brinjals bulk things out at 2–3 g net per 100 g.
