**Short answer:** Yes, in moderation. In Banting (*The Real Meal Revolution*), peanuts and natural peanut butter sit on the **Orange list** (eat with caution). Two tablespoons (32 g) of no-sugar peanut butter have about 3.2 g net carbs, fitting well within a daily budget of 25–50 g.

| Per 100 g | Total carbs | Fibre | Net carbs | Fat | Protein | Banting List |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-----------|-----|---------|--------------|
| Peanut butter, natural, no sugar | 16 g | 6 g | 10 g | 50 g | 25 g | Orange (Caution) |

A 2 tbsp (32 g) serve has about 5.1 g total carbs, 1.9 g fibre and **3.2 g net carbs**. (On South African labels, check "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" — natural jars list ~8–10g per 100g).

Source: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026. SA label spot-checks, Sep 2026.

## What to watch for

The jar matters more than the peanut. Sweetened versions sold in SA supermarkets run 18–22 g total carbs per 100 g, and the difference is nearly all sugar. I scanned a mainstream smooth jar in Banting Buddy last month and it flagged icing sugar and maltodextrin in the ingredients — two things you'd never taste outright, but they push a sandwich's worth of spread from 3 g to nearly 8 g net carbs.

Read the ingredients, not the front label. You want peanuts and salt, nothing else. "No added sugar" is a good sign; "reduced fat" usually isn't, because they replace the fat with starch and sugar. At Pick n Pay and Checkers, the house-brand natural jars (around R45–R60 for 400 g) are normally just peanuts and salt. Woolworths' natural range is similar but closer to R80. Whatever you buy, check the label again in Sep 2026 — recipes change without warning.

Because peanuts are on the Orange list, portions are the second trap. A level 2 tbsp serve is fine. Eating it from the jar with a spoon while you cook is how 3 g becomes 10 g. If you know you're a jar-snacker, portion it into a small bowl first. One slice of banting seed bread with a thin spread stays under 5 g net carbs. Two thick sandwiches plus a banana does not, and that's where people stall.

## What to eat instead

If peanuts don't agree with you or you want lower carbs, macadamia butter is the ultimate Banting choice at roughly 5 g net carbs per 100 g, though a small jar can cost over R150. Almond butter sits between the two at about 9 g net per 100 g. For a cheap crunch, I'd just eat a small handful of dry-roasted macadamias or walnuts instead of spreading anything at all.

Tahini is another option for savoury cooking at around 8 g net per 100 g. Skip "peanut-flavoured" spreads and chocolate nut spreads — most are 40–55 g net carbs per 100 g, basically dessert.
