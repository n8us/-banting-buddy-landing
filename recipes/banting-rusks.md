# Banting rusks recipe

**Short answer:** These Banting rusks use almond flour, coconut flour, psyllium and butter, are baked as a loaf, sliced, then dried low and slow. One batch makes **24 rusks at about 2g net carbs each**. They crunch like a rusk, not like a cake.

Rusks are a South African institution, and giving them up is one of the hardest parts of starting Banting. This version keeps the coffee-dipping ritual without the 15g of carbs.

## Ingredients

- 250g almond flour
- 50g coconut flour
- 30g psyllium husk
- 2 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 100g butter, softened
- 3 large eggs
- 60ml full-cream cream
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 30g erythritol (optional)

Optional: 1 tsp ground cinnamon, 50g chopped pecans, 30g desiccated coconut.

## Method

1. **Heat the oven** to 180°C and line a 20cm x 10cm loaf tin.
2. **Mix the dry ingredients** — almond flour, coconut flour, psyllium, baking powder, salt and any cinnamon.
3. **Beat the butter and sweetener** until soft, then add the eggs one at a time, followed by the cream and vanilla.
4. **Combine** the wet and dry mixtures. The dough will be stiff and slightly sticky.
5. **Rest for 10 minutes**, then press into the tin and smooth the top.
6. **Bake for 40 minutes** until golden and firm. Cool completely in the tin — at least 30 minutes.
7. **Slice** into 24 fingers, about 2cm thick.
8. **Dry** the slices on a rack at 100°C for 2.5–3 hours, turning once halfway. They are ready when they feel hard and light.
9. **Cool completely** before storing, or they will sweat and go soft.

## Why the double bake matters

A rusk is a twice-baked bread. The first bake sets the loaf; the second dries it into something hard enough to dunk. If you skip the drying stage you have low-carb cake fingers, which are nice but not rusks. Dry at 100°C, not higher — almond flour burns before it dries.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rusks are soft in the middle | Dry 30–45 minutes longer at 100°C |
| Crumbly when slicing | Cool the loaf completely; use a serrated knife |
| Too sweet | Reduce erythritol to 1 tbsp or omit |
| Burned bottoms | Use the middle rack and turn halfway |

## Variations

- **Buttermilk:** replace the cream with 60ml full-cream milk mixed with 1 tsp lemon juice.
- **Seedy:** add 50g sunflower and 30g pumpkin seeds.
- **Cinnamon pecan:** add 1 tsp cinnamon and 60g chopped pecans.
- **Chocolate:** add 30g cocoa powder and 20g extra sweetener.

## Storage

Store in an airtight tin for up to 3 weeks. They freeze well for up to 3 months — thaw at room temperature, not in the microwave, or they go chewy.

## What to serve with them

Black coffee, rooibos with a splash of cream, or tea. Two rusks is a portion. They also work as a [Banting snack]({{ site.baseurl }}/recipes/snacks/) with cheese.

## FAQ

### Are Banting rusks really low-carb?

Yes, at about 2g net carbs each. The carbs come from almond flour, coconut flour and cream, not from sugar or wheat. Two rusks is around 4g net carbs.

### Why are my rusks not crunchy?

They need longer in the drying stage. Return them to 100°C for another 30 minutes. Humidity also matters — cool them completely before sealing the tin.

### Can I use only almond flour?

Yes, use 300g almond flour and skip the coconut flour, but add 1 extra egg. The rusks will be slightly denser.
