# Banting seed crackers recipe

**Short answer:** These Banting seed crackers are a mix of sunflower, pumpkin, flax and chia seeds held together with psyllium husk. One batch makes about **40 crackers at 0.5g net carbs each** — crunchy enough for cheese, pâté and avocado.

They are the single most useful thing to have in the cupboard on Banting. Shop-bought "seed crackers" are often mostly wheat flour; these are not. The method is a two-stage bake: hot to set, low to dry.

## Ingredients

- 100g sunflower seeds
- 50g pumpkin seeds
- 50g flaxseeds
- 30g chia seeds
- 30g psyllium husk
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 250ml water
- 2 tbsp olive oil

Optional: 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tbsp sesame seeds, 1 tbsp chopped rosemary.

## Method

1. **Heat the oven** to 160°C and line a large baking tray with baking paper.
2. **Mix the dry ingredients** in a bowl — seeds, psyllium, salt and any spices.
3. **Add the water and oil.** Stir until there is no dry seed left. The mixture will look wet and slightly slimy; that is the psyllium working.
4. **Rest for 10 minutes.** The mixture thickens into a spreadable paste.
5. **Spread thinly.** Tip onto the tray and spread with a spatula to about 3–4mm thick. Thinner crackers crisp faster; thicker ones stay chewy in the middle.
6. **Score.** Cut into 4cm squares with a knife or pizza cutter. Do not cut all the way through.
7. **Bake at 160°C for 45 minutes.** The surface should look dry and the edges lift slightly.
8. **Dry at 120°C for 30–45 minutes.** Flip the whole sheet or break into crackers and return to the oven until crisp. Check every 10 minutes near the end — seeds burn quickly.
9. **Cool completely** before storing. They crisp up further as they cool.

## Why they hold together

Psyllium husk is the binder. Chia helps, but chia alone gives a softer cracker. If you skip the psyllium, you get a tray of loose seeds. Flaxseed meal also helps; whole flax is crunchier but less binding.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Crackers are chewy | They need longer at 120°C; dry until they snap |
| Mixture too wet | Add 1 tbsp psyllium and rest 5 minutes |
| Burned edges | Move the tray lower, or reduce to 150°C |
| Crackers stuck to paper | Bake 5 minutes longer before flipping; use a silicone mat |

## Variations

- **Smoked paprika and rosemary:** add 1 tsp each to the dry mix.
- **Sesame and sea salt:** swap 30g of sunflower seeds for sesame seeds.
- **Cheese crackers:** add 40g grated parmesan to the dry mix and reduce salt.
- **Thicker rounds:** press into a muffin tin for cracker cups.

## Storage

Cool completely, then store in an airtight jar for up to 3 weeks. If they go soft, return them to a 120°C oven for 10 minutes. They freeze well for up to 3 months.

## How to eat them

With cream cheese, cheddar, avocado, liver pâté, smoked salmon, tuna mayo or biltong. They also work as a base for canapés. See the [Banting snacks]({{ site.baseurl }}/recipes/snacks/) page for more pairings.

## FAQ

### Are seed crackers Banting?

Yes, if they are made from seeds and psyllium rather than wheat flour. Check shop-bought versions: wheat flour, rice flour, starch and sugar are common.

### How many carbs in a seed cracker?

About 0.5g net carbs per cracker in this recipe. Five crackers is around 2.5g net carbs.

### Can I make them without psyllium?

They will not hold together properly. Use ground flaxseed as a partial substitute, but add it to the dry mix and expect a softer cracker.
