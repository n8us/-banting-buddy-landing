# Banting Food List for Beginners

Eat meat, fish, eggs, most veg that grows above ground, and healthy natural fats freely (Green list). Add small, controlled portions of nuts, seeds, berries, and full-fat dairy (Orange list). Skip sugar, grains, seed oils, potatoes, and high-carb fruit (Red list).

That simple framework clears up most confusion. Many Banting followers aim for around 25g to 50g net carbs a day, spread across meals.

## Green list: eat freely (to satiety)

These are your daily foundation foods. They contain less than 5g carbs per 100g, keep blood sugar stable, and satisfy hunger naturally.

Meat covers beef, lamb, pork, chicken (skin-on thighs are best), game meat, boerewors made with zero added rusk or cereal filler, and biltong with no sugar coating. Fish covers snoek, hake, pilchards, trout, and tinned tuna in brine or olive oil. Eggs prepared any way you like. Natural fats cover real butter, ghee, extra virgin olive oil, avocado oil, coconut oil, lard, tallow, and duck fat.

Veg that works every day: spinach, swiss chard, cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, courgettes (baby marrows), green beans, asparagus, peppers, mushrooms, cucumber, and salad leaves. Full-fat dairy with minimal carbs: hard cheeses (cheddar, gouda, parmesan), butter, and fresh cream.

## Orange list: small, tracked portions only

These foods contain between 6g and 25g carbs per 100g. They are real, nutrient-rich whole foods, but the carbs add up quickly. If your goal is weight loss, limit these to small portions or keep them for weight maintenance.

- **Nuts and seeds:** Macadamias, pecans, and walnuts are lowest in net carbs. Almonds and unsweetened peanuts are fine in a strict 30g handful. Chia and flaxseed work well in porridge or crackers.
- **Vegetables:** Butternut, carrots, beetroot, sweet potatoes, peas, and onions. Keep cooked portions to half a cup.
- **Fruit:** Fresh berries only — strawberries, raspberries, and blackberries are best; blueberries have more sugar. Keep to a quarter cup.
- **Dairy with lactose:** Plain full-cream yoghurt, sour cream, and milk. A splash of full-cream milk in tea is fine, but drinking glasses of milk will stall weight loss.

I learned this one at Pick n Pay in Sep 2026. A jar marked "healthy" peanut butter listed sugar and maltodextrin after peanuts on the label. The scanner in the app flagged it straight away. The no-added-sugar jar next to it had peanuts and salt only. Same shelf, very different choice.

## Light red list: hardly ever (treats and bakes)

In Banting, gluten-free baking flours (almond flour, coconut flour) and sugar-free sweeteners (xylitol, erythritol, stevia) sit on the Light Red list. They make low-carb muffins or bread possible, but they can trigger sweet cravings and stall progress if eaten every day. Treat them as occasional bakes, not meal bases.

## Red list: strictly avoid

The Red list covers foods high in carbs, refined starches, sugar, or inflammatory industrial seed oils:

- **Sugar in all forms:** White sugar, brown sugar, honey, golden syrup, agave, jam, sweets, and soft drinks.
- **Grains and cereals:** Wheat, bread, rolls, wraps, pizza bases, pasta, oats, rice, corn, mielie meal (pap), and samp.
- **Starchy vegetables:** White potatoes, potato chips, and crisps.
- **Industrial seed and vegetable oils:** Sunflower oil, canola oil, corn oil, soybean oil, and margarine spreads. (Banting insists on natural fats like butter, lard, and olive oil).
- **Processed meats with fillers:** Cheaper braaiwors, polony, and viennas containing rusk, wheat flour, or sugar.
- **Sweet drinks and alcohol:** Beer, commercial ciders, sweetened liqueurs, and fruit juices.
- **Commercial "low-fat" products:** Diet yoghurts and dressings where natural fat is swapped for thickeners, starch, or sugar.

One banana can use half your day's carb budget. A bowl of oats does the same before you even add milk.

## How to shop without reading every label twice

Start with the outside perimeter at Woolworths, Checkers, or Pick n Pay. Meat, fresh fish, eggs, green vegetables, and real butter live there. Then pop into the aisles for tinned fish in olive oil or brine, raw nuts, coffee, and pure spices.

A practical rule: if a vegetable or whole food has under 5g carbs per 100g, it is Green list. If it has between 6g and 25g, treat it as Orange list and measure your portion. On South African packaged foods, check the "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" line — that is already your net carbs!
