# Banting food list

**Short answer:** eat freely from the green list — meat, fish, eggs, above-ground vegetables, full-fat dairy and natural fats. Keep the orange list to measured portions — nuts, berries, root vegetables, milk and legumes. Skip the red list — sugar, grains, industrial seed oils, potatoes and anything ultra-processed.

## What green, orange and red mean

Banting uses a traffic-light system because counting every gram is not realistic in a shop aisle. The colours are a shortcut: green is your daily base, orange needs a portion decision, red is a no.

| Band | Net carbs per 100g | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Green | 0–5g | Eat freely, to satiety. This is the foundation of fat adaptation. |
| Orange | 6–25g | Nutritious but carb-dense. One measured portion, not a plateful. |
| Red | 25g+ or refined | Skip. Sugar, grains, starch and ultra-processed food. |

Most people aim for 25–50g net carbs a day. Cooked meals should land around 5–10g a serving, which is why the [Banting recipes]({{ site.baseurl }}/recipes/) on this site list net carbs per serving rather than per 100g.

## Green list: eat freely

Green foods are under about 5g net carbs per 100g. They have little effect on blood sugar and you do not need to weigh them.

- **Leafy greens and low-carb vegetables:** spinach, kale, Swiss chard, rocket, watercress, lettuce, cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, asparagus, courgettes (baby marrows), cucumber, celery, green beans, peppers, mushrooms, aubergine, tomatoes, onions, leeks, radishes, gem squash, olives, avocado.
- **Meat and poultry:** beef, lamb, pork, chicken, turkey, duck, venison, goat. Skin on, fat left on.
- **Organ meat:** liver, kidney, heart. Cheap, dense in nutrients, and almost zero carb.
- **Fish and seafood:** snoek, hake, salmon, mackerel, sardines, pilchards, trout, tuna, prawns, calamari, mussels, oysters.
- **Eggs:** any style, preferably pasture-raised.
- **Naturally cured meat with no sugar:** biltong, bacon, pancetta, prosciutto, parma ham, sugar-free salami. Check the label — many SA brands add dextrose or maltodextrin.
- **Fats and oils:** butter, ghee, extra virgin olive oil, cold-pressed avocado oil, coconut oil, macadamia oil, beef tallow, duck fat, lard.
- **Hard and aged cheese:** cheddar, gouda, parmesan, pecorino, emmental.
- **Seeds:** flax, chia, pumpkin, sesame, sunflower.
- **Fermented foods:** homemade bone broth, raw sauerkraut, kimchi, sugar-free fermented pickles, unsweetened coconut yoghurt, milk kefir.
- **Drinks and condiments:** water, sparkling water, rooibos and herbal teas, black coffee, apple cider vinegar, wine vinegar with no sugar, tamari, herbs and spices, sugar-free avocado-oil mayonnaise.

Is biltong actually green? Usually — but check the rub. [Is biltong Banting friendly?]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/is-biltong-banting-friendly/) covers the sugar coating problem.

## Orange list: measured portions

Orange foods are 6–25g net carbs per 100g. They are real whole foods, not junk — but the carbs add up fast. One closed handful or a quarter cup is the usual portion.

- **Full-fat dairy:** brie, camembert, feta, mozzarella, ricotta, blue cheese, cottage cheese, cream, cream cheese, sour cream, full-fat Greek yoghurt, a splash of full-cream milk, unsweetened almond or coconut milk.
- **Root vegetables:** beetroot, butternut, pumpkin, carrots, celeriac, parsnip, sweet potato, garden peas, spaghetti squash.
- **Fruit:** berries first — strawberries, raspberries, blackberries, blueberries, gooseberries. Higher sugar: apple, banana, pear, grapes, mango, pineapple, watermelon, granadilla.
- **Nuts and nut butters:** almonds, macadamias, walnuts, pecans, hazelnuts, Brazil nuts, pistachios, peanuts, 100% nut butter with no added sugar. Cashews are the outlier — closer to red.
- **Legumes:** chickpeas, lentils, kidney beans, cannellini beans, black-eyed beans, edamame. Maintenance only for most people.
- **Caffeinated drinks:** coffee, black tea, green tea, oolong. Plain, no sugar.
- **Kombucha and water kefir:** low-sugar only. Check the label for added sugar.

Carb traps in this group: [is peanut butter Banting friendly?]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/is-peanut-butter-banting-friendly/) and [is sweet potato Banting friendly?]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/is-sweet-potato-banting-friendly/) explain where the numbers move.

## Red list: skip

Red foods are high-carb, refined, or ultra-processed. There is no portion that fits a low-carb day.

- **Sugar in all forms:** white, brown and icing sugar, honey, golden syrup, agave, jam, sweets, chocolate spread, commercial ice cream.
- **Grains and cereals:** wheat, bread, rolls, wraps, croissants, pasta, pizza crust, oats, muesli, granola, corn flakes, instant porridge, rice, couscous, barley, rye, spelt.
- **Starchy vegetables and starches:** white potatoes, chips, crisps, mielie meal (pap), samp, corn.
- **Industrial seed and vegetable oils:** sunflower, canola, corn, soybean, cottonseed, safflower, rice bran, margarine and shortening.
- **Processed meat with filler:** polony, viennas, hot dogs, cheaper boerewors and braaiwors made with rusk or wheat flour.
- **Sugary and diet drinks:** soft drinks, fruit juice, iced tea, energy drinks, flavoured milk, milkshakes.
- **Commercial sauces and dressings:** ketchup, sweet chilli, marinades and salad dressings made with sugar and seed oil.
- **"Low-fat" and "Banting" packaged products:** diet yoghurt, low-fat dressing, and plenty of products with "Banting" on the front that carry 15–20g carbs a portion.

## How to read a South African label

On an SA nutrition panel, **"Glycaemic Carbohydrate" is already net carbs** — fibre is listed separately and has been deducted. You do not need to subtract fibre again. Check separately for sugar alcohols (maltitol, xylitol, erythritol); they are handled differently by different manufacturers.

The ingredient list matters more than the carb line. If sugar, dextrose, maltodextrin, glucose syrup, modified starch or wheat flour appear in the first five ingredients, it is not a green food no matter what the front of the pack says. More detail in [how to count net carbs]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/how-to-count-net-carbs/).

Two South African traps worth knowing:

- **Boerewors** is often bulked with rusk or cereal. Ask the butcher for a filler-free blend or read the pack.
- **Biltong and droëwors** are frequently cured with sugar. The rub is where the carbs hide.

## Shop and cook from the list

Start on the outside of the supermarket — meat, fish, eggs, dairy, fresh vegetables, butter. Then go in for tinned fish in olive oil, raw nuts, coffee and pure spices. That loop gets you most of the green list in one trip.
