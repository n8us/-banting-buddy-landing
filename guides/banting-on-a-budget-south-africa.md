# Banting on a Budget in South Africa

You can eat Banting for about R800 to R1000 a week for one person in 2026. Base meals on eggs, chicken thighs, mince, tinned fish, cabbage, and spinach. (Remember: on South African food labels, "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" already gives you net carbs).

Fancy steaks and imported "keto" snacks push costs up. Basic braai meat, eggs, and local green veg keep them down. Your carb target does not change, only where you buy the food.

Cheapest days are eggs, thighs and pilchards. Mince twice a week keeps flavour up without breaking the budget. Biltong is a treat snack, not a base meat.

## A R900 trolley that lasts a week

I priced this at Pick n Pay, Sep 2026. Eggs 30s R95, thighs 1.5kg R134, mince 1kg R139, pilchards x2 R64, tuna x2 R52, bacon 200g R55, butter 500g R135, cream 250ml R42, cheddar 400g R85, cabbage R22, spinach 2 bunches R36, cauliflower x2 R45, salad bag R35, avo x2 R30, peanuts 500g R45. Total near R970. Checkers came out R40 cheaper on the same day when I swapped butter and cheese for house brands.

Cook once, eat twice. Roast thighs Sunday, eat cold Monday lunch. Brown 1kg mince with cabbage Tuesday, pack half for Wednesday. Boil six eggs for grab snacks. Soup from bones and leftover veg stretches to Friday.

Veg to favour: cabbage, spinach, gem squash, green beans when on special, frozen cauli rice when fresh is dear. Fruit: stick to half an avo or a handful of frozen berries. A banana looks cheap at R3, but at 23.8g net per medium fruit (USDA Sep 2026: 22.8g total, 2.6g fibre per 100g) it blows your day.

## Store habits that save Rands

Shop with a list from the [7-day Banting meal plan]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/7-day-banting-meal-plan/). It was built to reuse leftovers.

Check the meat markdown fridge first. I got pork chops at 30% off at 9am at Checkers, froze them the same day. Tinned fish in brine or oil beats tomato sauce versions with added sugar. Plain full-cream yoghurt in 1kg tubs costs half per 100g of little flavoured cups.

Woolworths is great for quality but brutal for budget Banting. I buy butter, cream and bulk nuts at Checkers or Pick n Pay, then only grab marked-down veg at Woolies if I pass one. Your favourite store may differ, prices shift by suburb.

Skip Banting-branded bars, breads and crackers till your budget allows. One R89 loaf with 3g net per slice is fine as a treat, but eggs at R3 each fill you more.
