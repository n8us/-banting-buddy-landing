# What is Banting?

<div class="answer-box">
<strong>Short answer:</strong> Banting is South Africa's low-carb, high-fat (LCHF) way of eating. It cuts sugar, refined starch and ultra-processed food, and replaces them with meat, fish, eggs, full-fat dairy, vegetables that grow above the ground, and natural fats. Most people aim for <strong>25–50g net carbs a day</strong>. It is a long-term lifestyle, not a calorie-counting diet.
</div>

You hear the word at braais, in school parking lots and in every second Checkers aisle. But "Banting" gets used loosely — sometimes for strict keto, sometimes for any low-carb product with a green label. This page explains what Banting actually means in South Africa, what you eat, how many carbs you get, and how to start without overcomplicating it.

## Where the name "Banting" comes from

Banting is named after William Banting, a London undertaker who struggled with obesity for most of his life. In 1862 his doctor put him on a diet that cut bread, sugar, beer and starchy food, and replaced them with meat, fish and fat. He lost a large amount of weight and published a pamphlet in 1863 called *Letter on Corpulence, Addressed to the Public*. It became one of the first popular diet books, and "banting" became a verb for dieting in parts of Europe.

The modern South African movement started in 2013, when sports scientist Prof Tim Noakes publicly backed a low-carb, high-fat diet after his own experience with insulin resistance. His profile, a very public professional hearing, and the fact that the diet fits South African food culture — meat, eggs, cheese, butter and braai — turned Banting into a mainstream movement rather than a niche experiment.

## How Banting works

Banting lowers the amount of carbohydrate you eat, which lowers the amount of glucose entering your blood. With less glucose around, your body leans more on fat for fuel — both fat from food and stored body fat. Protein and fat also keep you full for longer, so most people eat less without counting calories.

A few honest points:

- **You do not have to be in ketosis.** Strict keto aims for 20–30g net carbs. Banting allows up to about 50g, and many people at that level are not in ketosis. That is fine. The goal is fewer carb spikes and steadier energy, not a urine test.
- **Fat is not unlimited.** Banting is low-carb, not a free-for-all. If weight loss stalls, portion size and total intake still matter.
- **It is not a cure.** Banting can improve weight, blood sugar and hunger, but it does not replace medical care or guarantee results.

## The five rules of Banting

Most South African Banting lists come back to the same five rules:

1. **Keep net carbs low.** Aim for 25–50g a day, with main meals around 5–10g. The stricter end suits weight loss and blood sugar control; the higher end suits maintenance.
2. **Eat whole foods.** Meat, fish, eggs, cheese, cream, butter, olive oil, avocado, nuts and vegetables that grow above the ground.
3. **Cut sugar and refined starch.** No bread, rice, pasta, pap, potatoes, cereal, jam, honey or fruit juice. This is the non-negotiable part.
4. **Add fat on purpose.** Butter on the veg, cream in the coffee, olive oil on the salad. Low-fat products usually replace fat with starch or sugar.
5. **Read the label.** In South Africa, "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" is already net carbs. Check the ingredients for sugar, dextrose, maltodextrin and starch.

A recipe does not need special "Banting flour" to qualify. A braaied lamb chop with a green salad is Banting. So is a cheese omelette. The packaged substitutes — almond flour bread, seed crackers, sugar-free desserts — are for when you want the texture, not because you need them.

## How many carbs a day on Banting?

The usual range is **25–50g net carbs a day**.

- **25–30g** is the stricter end. It suits people who want steady weight loss or who are managing blood sugar, and it is close to keto.
- **30–50g** is the common Banting zone. It leaves room for berries, nuts, full-fat yoghurt and a wider range of vegetables.
- **50g+** is maintenance for some people, but it is no longer strict Banting. If progress stalls, tighten the ceiling before you change anything else.

Most main meals should sit at **5–10g net carbs**. That is why the [Banting recipes]({{ site.baseurl }}/recipes/) on this site list carbs per serving rather than per 100g.

### What "net carbs" means on a South African label

On South African packaged food, the label lists **"Glycaemic Carbohydrate"** (sometimes "Available Carbohydrate"). Under SA labelling rules, dietary fibre is already excluded and listed separately. **That number is already your net carbs.** Do not subtract fibre again.

On imported US products and whole-food databases, the table shows **"Total Carbohydrate"**, which includes fibre. There the formula is:

**Net carbs = total carbohydrate − dietary fibre**

The full method, including the polyol rules and the traps, is in [how to count net carbs]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/how-to-count-net-carbs/).

## What to eat on Banting

The colour bands are a shortcut so you do not have to weigh every gram in the shop. South African Banting uses **five bands, not three**: green, orange, coral (also called light red), grey and red. These are the headline foods; the [full Banting food list]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/banting-food-list-beginners/) breaks down every band with net carbs per 100g.

### Green list: eat freely

Green foods are roughly 0–5g net carbs per 100g.

| Food | Net carbs per 100g | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Beef, lamb, pork, chicken | 0g | Fat on, skin on |
| Eggs | about 0.7g | Any style |
| Snoek, hake, pilchards, sardines | 0g | Check sauces for sugar |
| Cheddar, gouda, parmesan | 1–4g | Hard, aged cheese |
| Spinach, cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli | 2–4g | Above-ground vegetables |
| Avocado | about 1.8g | Half a fruit is a normal portion |
| Butter, ghee, olive oil | 0g | Natural fats only |
| Biltong, plain | 2–4g | Check the rub for sugar |

### Orange list: measured portions

Orange foods are roughly 6–25g net carbs per 100g. They are real food, not junk, but the carbs add up.

- **Berries:** strawberries, raspberries, blueberries — a small handful.
- **Nuts:** almonds, macadamias, walnuts, pecans — one closed handful.
- **Root vegetables:** butternut, pumpkin, carrots, beetroot, sweet potato — a quarter-cup portion.
- **Full-fat dairy:** full-cream milk, Greek yoghurt, cottage cheese, cream.
- **Legumes:** lentils, chickpeas and beans are allowed in a measured portion — about half a cup, not a bowl. They sit on the orange list, not the red list.

### Coral list (light red): hardly ever

Coral is the band the old three-colour lists leave out. It covers whole, unrefined foods that are still high in carbs: gluten-free grains, alternative flours (rice, corn, potato), natural sugars such as honey, maple and dates, and high-sugar whole foods. Think 26g+ net carbs per 100g in a whole food. Maintenance only, not daily.

### Grey area: your call

Low-carb bars, "Banting" breads, sugar-free sweets, sweeteners and alcohol are neither a food group nor a carb band. They are processed substitutes. Use them occasionally if they keep you on plan, not as the base of your diet. Most stalls I see come from treating grey products as green ones.

### Red list: skip it

Sugar, bread, pasta, rice, pap, potatoes, cereal, fruit juice, cold drinks, beer, industrial seed oils (sunflower, canola, corn, margarine) and anything ultra-processed. This is the list that does the heavy lifting.

## What a day of Banting looks like

A normal day does not need special ingredients. This sample lands at about 24g net carbs and leaves room for a piece of fruit or a bigger portion at supper.

| Meal | Example | Net carbs |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 2 eggs, 2 rashers of bacon, spinach, coffee with cream | about 2g |
| Lunch | Roast chicken thigh, half an avocado, salad, olive oil | about 5g |
| Snack | 30g plain biltong and 25g cheddar | about 2g |
| Dinner | 2 boerewors, cauliflower mash with butter, green beans | about 9g |
| Dessert | 100g full-fat Greek yoghurt with 50g berries | about 6g |
| **Total** | | **about 24g** |

Two checks matter here. Boerewors often contains rusk or breadcrumbs — read the label or buy from a butcher who lists the ingredients. And "cauliflower mash" only stays low-carb if you do not add a pile of starchy thickener.

## Banting vs keto vs LCHF

| | Banting | Keto | LCHF general |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily net carbs | 25–50g | 20–30g | 50–100g |
| Method | Food lists and whole foods | Macro tracking | Flexible low carb |
| Fats | Natural fats; no seed oils | High fat; some allow seed oils | Natural fats preferred |
| Fruit | Berries only | Berries only, weighed | Berries plus occasional apple or plum |
| Sweeteners | Occasional | Common | Similar to Banting |

Banting is not simply "keto with a different name". At 50g many people are not in ketosis, and Banting is stricter about food quality and seed oils than casual keto. It is also easier to run in a family kitchen because you use food lists instead of weighing everything. The full comparison is in [Banting vs keto vs LCHF]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/banting-vs-keto-vs-lchf/).

## Does Banting work?

For weight loss, low-carb diets perform as well as — and in some trials slightly better than — conventional low-fat diets over 6–12 months. The bigger predictor is whether you can stick to it. Banting tends to do well in South Africa because the food is familiar and you do not need to buy imports.

For blood sugar, cutting carbs lowers glucose and insulin load. That is why many people with type 2 diabetes see better readings. If you take insulin or sulfonylureas, this is exactly why you need your doctor involved — your doses may need to change.

For heart health, the picture is mixed. Low-carb eating often raises HDL (the "good" cholesterol) and lowers triglycerides. LDL (the "bad" cholesterol) responds differently from person to person: some people see it fall, some see it rise. Banting tends to dismiss that variation. Do not. Get a lipid panel before and after, and discuss it with your doctor.

For hunger, the advantage is real. Fat and protein are more satiating than refined carbs, so most people stop snacking between meals without trying.

What Banting will not do: override a calorie surplus, fix poor sleep, or make processed "Banting" products healthy. It is a framework, not a magic switch.

## Seven common Banting mistakes

1. **Not eating enough fat.** You end up hungry by 10am and raid the biscuit tin. Add butter, olive oil, avocado or cheese to meals.
2. **Fearing salt.** Low-carb eating flushes water and sodium. Weak, headachy and dizzy in week one usually means water and a pinch of salt, not failure.
3. **Drinking your carbs.** Fruit juice, flavoured lattes, craft beer and "sugar-free" cordials with maltodextrin.
4. **Trusting the front of the pack.** "Low carb", "keto" and "Banting-friendly" are marketing terms. Read the nutrition table and the ingredients.
5. **Too much protein, too little veg.** A plate of meat alone gets boring and constipating. Add above-ground vegetables and fat.
6. **Quitting at week two.** The first week is water weight and adjustment. Fat adaptation takes longer.
7. **Not planning.** Banting fails at 7pm when there is nothing in the fridge. Plan three repeat meals and shop for them.

## How to start Banting: your first seven days

1. **Clear the kitchen.** Remove bread, cereal, rice, pasta, sugar, potatoes, crisps and juice. Give them away rather than leaving them in the cupboard.
2. **Shop the outside of the supermarket.** Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, vegetables, butter. Then go in for tinned fish, nuts, coffee and spices.
3. **Pick three breakfasts, three lunches and three dinners** you already like. Repeat them for a week. Variety can wait.
4. **Learn one label.** Find "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" per 100g and check the ingredients for sugar, dextrose, maltodextrin and starch.
5. **Expect a rough 3–5 days.** Headache, tiredness and irritability are common. Drink water, salt your food, and do not train hard.
6. **Walk daily.** A 20-minute walk helps mood, sleep and insulin sensitivity without adding hunger.
7. **Review at day seven.** Energy, hunger, digestion and weight. Adjust the carb ceiling rather than abandoning the plan.

## Who should talk to a doctor first

Banting is generally safe for healthy adults, but get medical advice first if you:

- take insulin or medication for type 2 diabetes (risk of low blood sugar)
- take blood pressure medication (doses may need adjusting)
- are pregnant or breastfeeding
- have kidney disease, gout, pancreatitis or a history of eating disorders
- have familial high cholesterol or existing heart disease
- are considering Banting for a child

This page is general information, not medical advice.

## Frequently asked questions

### What exactly is the Banting diet?

Banting is a South African low-carb, high-fat (LCHF) eating plan. It removes sugar, refined starch and processed food, and builds meals around meat, fish, eggs, full-fat dairy, above-ground vegetables and natural fats, with a daily net carb target of about 25–50g.

### How many carbs can I eat on Banting?

Most people aim for 25–50g net carbs a day. Weight loss and blood sugar control usually work better at the lower end. Maintenance can sit closer to 50g. Main meals should land around 5–10g.

### What is the difference between Banting and keto?

Keto is stricter, usually 20–30g net carbs a day, and relies on macro tracking. Banting allows up to about 50g and uses food lists instead of weighing everything. Banting is also stricter about whole foods and industrial seed oils. You can follow Banting without being in ketosis.

### What can I eat on Banting?

Meat, fish, eggs, hard cheese, full-fat dairy, butter, olive oil, avocado, nuts, legumes in measured portions, and vegetables that grow above the ground. Berries and root vegetables are limited portions. Sugar, gluten grains, potatoes, fruit juice, seed oils and ultra-processed food are out.

### Are legumes allowed on Banting?

Yes, in measured portions. Lentils, chickpeas and beans sit on the orange list, not the red list. Half a cup is the usual serving; a large bowl will still blow the carb budget. Check tinned beans for added sugar.

### Is fruit allowed on Banting?

Berries are the main fruit: strawberries, raspberries, blueberries and blackberries, in small portions. Bananas, grapes, mangoes and other high-sugar fruit are effectively off the menu. See [is banana Banting friendly?]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/is-banana-banting-friendly/)

### Can I drink alcohol on Banting?

Dry wine and spirits with soda are the least bad options, occasionally. Beer is high-carb, and sweet mixers, ciders and dessert wines are out. Alcohol also lowers your resolve and disrupts sleep, so it is not a daily habit.

### Do I need to count calories on Banting?

No. Most people eat less because fat and protein are filling. But Banting is not a licence for unlimited fat: if weight loss stalls, look at portions, nuts, cheese and "Banting" products before cutting anything else.

### How fast will I lose weight on Banting?

The first week often drops 1–3kg, much of it water as glycogen stores fall. After that, 0.5–1kg a week is a realistic range for many people. Results vary with starting weight, activity, sleep and how strictly you keep the carb ceiling.

### Is Banting healthy?

For many people it improves weight, blood sugar, triglycerides and hunger. The long-term evidence is still evolving, and LDL cholesterol rises in some people. If you have existing health conditions or take medication, work with your doctor rather than self-experimenting.

### Is Banting the same as Atkins?

They are close cousins. Atkins uses numbered phases with a very low starting carb limit and increases carbs over time. Banting uses colour food lists and a steady 25–50g range. Both cut sugar and refined starch.

## The bottom line

Banting is South Africa's practical version of low-carb, high-fat eating: whole food, natural fat, vegetables, and a carb ceiling low enough to change how your body runs. It does not need special products, and it does not require ketosis. Pick a carb target, learn the five colour bands, plan a week of repeat meals, and give it a month before you judge it.
