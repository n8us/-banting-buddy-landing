# FB Drop Kit — profile funnel version

Internal page. Not linked anywhere. Don't share this URL.

**How this works now:** comments carry zero links (groups never filter them). Your profile is the funnel — anyone who clicks your name after a helpful comment finds your site. Set the profile up once, then just paste comments.

---

## Set up once: your profile funnel

On your Facebook personal profile:

1. **Edit public details → Website** — enter exactly:
   ```
   https://n8us.github.io/-banting-buddy-landing/go/
   ```
   (That's a short redirect — installs still get attributed to `fb-profile` in Play Console.)

2. **Bio** (under your name, 101 chars max) — use something like:
   ```
   I break down banting carbs for SA supermarket foods. Lists on my site below
   ```

3. **Don't** put the URL in your profile photo. A URL in a photo isn't tappable, looks spammy, and undoes the trust the comments build. Keep the photo as your normal face — that's what makes the profile read as a real person, which is the whole point.

4. Optional: your **cover photo** can be a screenshot of the food list page or anything banting-related. Visual proof, still no URL needed.

After this: every helpful comment you leave is a permanent, clickable funnel. No links in comments ever again.

---

## The rules (read once, save yourself a ban)

1. **Max 2 comments per group per day.** Never dump the whole kit in one sitting.
2. **Zero links in comments.** The answer lives in the comment text itself. If someone wants more, they tap your name — the profile does the rest.
3. **Don't post the same comment twice in one group.** Each topic goes once. If it flops after a week, reword it — never copy-paste identical text.
4. **Reply to every reply.** Threads with replies get shown to more people.
5. **If a comment is deleted, leave the group alone for 3 days.** Don't retry the same day.
6. **App pitch only when someone asks** about apps/tools/trackers. Never lead with it.
7. **Never say "link in bio" inside the comment.** That phrase is flagged as influencer-spam in many groups. Just answer well. If someone asks "where can I get the full list", reply naturally: *"I keep the full version on my site — it's linked in my profile."* (No URL in the reply either.)

## How tracking works

- Profile funnel: `…/go/` → redirects to the site tagged `utm_source=fb-profile`. Installs from profile visitors show as **fb-profile** in Play Console → Acquisition → Campaigns.
- All profile traffic lands in one bucket (you can't tell which group it came from — judge groups by the replies/likes you see in-group).
- Appendix at the bottom has direct tracked links for the rare group that allows links in comments — those keep per-topic attribution.
- GoatCounter shows click volume in near-real-time; Play Console shows installs a day or two later.

## Group 1: Banting for Beginners

<https://www.facebook.com/groups/bantingforbeginners>

### Comment 1 — biltong

Post when: someone asks about biltong, snacks, or protein on the go (or in an existing biltong thread).

```
Yes — plain beef biltong is one of the best banting snacks there is. ~2–4g net carbs per 100g and about 50g protein. The trap is the sweet marinades (honey, BBQ, sweet chilli) — those can hit 12–18g carbs. Always check the ingredients list for brown sugar or maltodextrin near the top.

Game (kudu, springbok) and ostrich follow the same rule: plain is low-carb, sweet coatings aren't.
```

### Comment 2 — food list for beginners

Post when: someone asks where to start, what to eat, or feels overwhelmed.

```
When I started I just focused on three things: meat/fish/eggs, veg that grows above ground, and real fats (butter, ghee, olive oil, avocado). Net carbs under 50g/day is the usual banting target.

One SA label tip that saves people: on local packaging, "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" is already net carbs — fibre is listed separately and already deducted. Don't subtract it again.
```

### Comment 3 — boerewors

Post when: someone asks about boerewors, braai meat, or wors brands.

```
Depends on the filler. Traditional pure wors (meat, fat, vinegar, spices) is ~1–3g net carbs per 100g. Commercial wors bulked out with rusk or wheat cereal can be 8–12g per serving. Check the ingredients — if rusk, wheat or sugar is near the top, skip it.
```

### Comment 4 — breakfast

Post when: someone asks about breakfast, or says they're bored of eggs.

```
Bacon and eggs obviously, but it doesn't have to stop there — omelettes, chia pudding with full-cream coconut milk, smoked salmon, even last night's leftovers. Most banting breakfasts land between 1–8g net carbs. The trick is protein + fat at breakfast; hunger stays away until lunch.
```

### Comment 5 — banana / fruit

Post when: someone asks about bananas, fruit, or sweet cravings.

```
Not on strict days — one medium banana is ~24g net carbs, basically your whole day's budget. Berries are the better option: a quarter cup of strawberries is ~2–3g. Fruit is the thing most people accidentally blow their carbs on.
```

### Comment 6 — snacks

Post when: someone asks about snacks, or "what do I snack on between meals".

```
More out there than people think — pork crackling (0g carbs), cheese cubes, boiled eggs, macadamias (portion them ~30g), full-fat yoghurt. A few packaged options at Woolies/Checkers/PnP actually pass too — most come in at 0–4g net carbs per serve. Check for hidden sugar in "health" bars and flavoured nuts.
```

### Comment 7 — "banting is too expensive"

Post when: someone complains about the cost of banting, especially meat prices.

```
It's the processed "banting products" that kill the budget, not the diet. Chicken thighs, mince, eggs, tinned pilchards, and seasonal veg are cheap. The expensive stuff (keto bars, banting bread, specialty cheeses) is all optional.
```

### Comment 8 — peanut butter

Post when: someone asks about peanut butter, nut butters, or sweet treats.

```
Yes, if it's the natural kind — 2 tablespoons of no-sugar peanut butter is ~3.2g net carbs. The trap is the sweetened jars (some popular SA brands add sugar and hydrogenated oil). Flip it over: if sugar or palm oil is in the first three ingredients, put it back.
```

### App comment (only when someone asks about apps/tools/trackers)

```
Full disclosure — I build Banting Buddy, so I'm obviously biased, but that's exactly why it exists. It tracks the 50g rule, scans barcodes at Checkers/Woolies/PnP offline (flags hidden sugars and maltodextrin), and your data stays on your phone — no account, no cloud. Free on Android — it's linked in my profile.
```

## Group 2: Banting 7 Day Meal Plans

<https://www.facebook.com/groups/Banting7DayMealPlans>

### Comment A — "meal plan please" / "what do I eat this week"

Post when: someone asks for a plan, or the weekly "what's for dinner" thread. Strongest content for this group.

```
A simple structure that works: pick a protein, a veg that grows above ground, and a real fat for each meal. Day of mince + cauliflower mash, day of chicken thighs + cabbage, day of lamb chops + brinjal. Build it that way for a week and you'll land under 50g net carbs without calculating every meal.

The thing that makes it stick: cook once, eat twice — dinner leftovers are tomorrow's lunch. Decision fatigue is what kills plans, not the food.
```

### Comment B — braai sides

Post when: someone asks what to make for a braai, or it's a weekend thread.

```
Braai is easy on banting — the meat was never the problem. It's the sides: pap, sheba, braaibroodjies, potato salad. Cauliflower mash, grilled brinjal, cabbage slaw, and a cheese-and-mushroom bake all work (2–7g net carbs per portion). Skip the sweet bastings and the beer and you're fine.
```

### Comment C — "how do I count net carbs?"

Post when: someone is confused about carbs, labels, or fibre subtraction.

```
Formula: net carbs = total carbs − fibre. On South African labels it's easier than that — "Glycaemic Carbohydrate" is already net carbs (fibre is listed separately and already deducted). Just watch for sugar alcohols in "low carb" products — those need subtracting manually.
```

### Comment D — "lunch/dinner ideas that aren't salads"

Post when: someone says they're bored, or asks for dinner ideas mid-week.

```
Leftovers done right — last night's lamb chops with cauliflower mash, omelettes with whatever's in the fridge, chicken thighs with cabbage and bacon. I keep coming back to the principle: protein + veg that grows above ground + real fat. If you plan that three times a week the rest fills itself.
```

### Comment E — "is this expensive to follow?"

Post when: budget comes up in a meal plan thread.

```
Depends on the proteins. Chicken thighs, mince, eggs and tinned fish keep a week of banting meals under a normal grocery budget — it's the artisanal banting products (keto bars, "banting" bread) that blow it out.
```

### App comment (only when someone asks about apps/trackers/planners)

```
Full disclosure — I'm the developer of Banting Buddy, so biased, but it has a meal planner and auto-builds the grocery list organised by aisle for SA stores. Works fully offline, no account. Free on Android — linked in my profile.
```

## After 48 hours — the only decision that matters

**Play Console → Acquisition → Campaigns:** look for `fb-profile` (and `dropkit` if you used appendix links).

- **Installs coming in?** Keep going, 2 comments per day. Look at which comments got replies/likes in-group — that's your topic signal now, since profile clicks merge into one bucket. Make more comments like the ones people respond to.
- **Comments getting likes/replies but no installs after ~100 profile clicks?** The funnel's broken somewhere — check the profile Website link still works, and that the bio mentions banting so people know why they're tapping.
- **Zero engagement on a topic after 4 drops?** Kill it. Don't retry.
- **One group clearly more engaged than the other?** Shift to 2 comments/day there, every-other-day on the weak one.

Cycle: 2 comments/day, read the room every 48h, cut the flat topics, write more of what gets replies. The kit has ~14 comments — by the time you run out, the data tells you which 5 to rewrite.
