# Banting Buddy vs generic carb counters

**Short answer:** If you eat Banting, a Banting-default app saves you daily setup. If you switch between keto, diabetic counting and coaching reports, a generic counter is more flexible.

Generic counters are built for everyone. Banting Buddy is built for one way of eating: around 50g net carbs a day, green/orange/red food lists, and label checks that make sense in a South African supermarket. That focus is either exactly what you want or a reason to pick something else.

| | Banting Buddy 2.0 | Generic carb counter |
|---|---|---|
| Carb default | ~50g net, fibre shown | Set it yourself to 50g net |
| Food lists | Green, orange and red lists built in | No lists, or keto lists you'll rename in your head |
| Barcode scan | Built in for quick net-carb checks | Often paywalled or capped on free tiers |
| Offline | Logs and lists work without signal | Log may cache, search and sync often need data |
| Meal ideas | Banting meals in app | Seldom, or plans that lean low-fat |
| Account | No account, stays on your phone | Account usual, cloud sync by default |
| Cost shape | Free with ads, all features included; optional ad removal for 1, 3 or 12 months | Free with caps, paid tiers open up scans and reports, check rands on the Play Store |

Source for carb maths: USDA FoodData Central, Sep 2026. Prices shift, so open the Play Store listing before you pay.

## Where Banting Buddy wins

Setup. You install it, your target is already ~50g net carbs with fibre subtracted, and the food language matches the [Banting food list for beginners]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/banting-food-list-beginners/). No macro maths on day one.

Aisle checks. You can pull out your phone at Checkers or Pick n Pay, scan a barcode and see net carbs plus flagged sugars without signal. I use it most for peanut butter, yoghurt and sauces — the three aisles where "healthy" labels lie fastest. Our [peanut butter question]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/is-peanut-butter-banting-friendly/) shows how a 3g serve becomes 8g when maltodextrin sneaks in.

Privacy and simplicity. No account, no password, no food log on a server. If that matters to you, it matters a lot. If you want coach sharing and web dashboards, it cuts the other way.

## Where generic counters win

Coaching and sync. If your dietitian wants your cloud log each Friday, or you live in Apple Health exports and recipe importers, a generic counter wins. Banting Buddy keeps logs on your phone, so sharing means screenshots.

Mixed eating. If you cook for a family where one person does strict keto (20-30g), one does Banting (50g) and kids eat normally, a generic counter with profiles is easier. You'd set carbs to 50g net, show fibre, and pin Banting swaps like cauli mash. Our [cauliflower carbs]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/how-many-carbs-in-cauliflower/) and [avocado carbs]({{ site.baseurl }}/questions/how-many-carbs-in-avocado/) pages show the per-100g maths you'll reuse.

Charts. Some folks love streaks, trend lines and big recipe feeds. That's fair — pick the tool you'll actually open.

## How to set either one up right

Whichever you pick, do this once: set carbs to net (total minus fibre), set the target near 50g, and show fibre on the log screen. Then read [how to count net carbs]({{ site.baseurl }}/guides/how-to-count-net-carbs/) so label maths stops surprising you. The most common setup trap is tracking total carbs against a net target, which makes avo look expensive and bread look nearly fine.
